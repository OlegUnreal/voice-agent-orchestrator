Title: Text-to-speech voices and prosody
Tags: text to speech, voices, prosody, latency, streaming
Updated: 2026-05-18

Speech synthesis is streamed sentence by sentence. The first audio frame is
returned as soon as the model has a complete clause, which keeps the caller
from hearing a gap between the agent's decision and its voice.

Twelve production voices ship across the supported languages, each with a
neutral register and a warmer conversational variant. Voice choice is a
per-trunk setting, not a per-call one, so a caller hears the same agent across
the whole conversation. Speaking rate is adjustable within a narrow band;
outside it the voices lose intelligibility on telephone audio.

Numbers, currency and phone strings are normalised by a text front end before
synthesis: an account identifier is spelled as digits, a money amount is read as
words, and a decimal separator is spoken explicitly. Embedding markup directly
in agent output is discouraged because it leaks into transcripts.

If synthesis fails mid-reply, the pipeline falls back to a compact tone-free
placeholder sentence and reports the incident, rather than leaving the caller in
silence.
