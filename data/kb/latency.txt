Title: Voice latency budget and p95 targets
Tags: latency, p95, budget, turn around time, performance
Updated: 2026-06-01

The end-to-end target is a 700 millisecond median and a 1200 millisecond p95
voice-to-voice turnaround: the interval between the caller finishing a sentence
and hearing the first audio frame of the reply.

The budget is allocated across the pipeline. Voice-activity detection closing an
utterance takes up to 400 milliseconds, the final transcript is available about
800 milliseconds after end of speech, the first model token is emitted 450
milliseconds after the prompt is assembled, and the first synthesis frame is
produced 280 milliseconds after the first complete clause.

Retrieval sits inside the prompt-assembly step and is budgeted at 30
milliseconds per turn. It is deliberately the cheapest stage in the path: the
index is in-process, the embedder is a fixed-width matrix multiply, and the
reranker is one dot product per candidate. If retrieval latency exceeds its
budget the agent still answers from whatever evidence arrived first.

Latency is measured per turn and published in the console by percentile, not by
average, because a mean hides the tail that callers actually notice. Regressions
are alerted at a 15 percent p95 rise sustained for 10 minutes.
