Title: Cost per call and unit economics
Tags: cost, unit economics, tokens, price per call, margin
Updated: 2026-05-28

A typical four-minute support call costs less than one US cent in model spend.
The breakdown is the part customers ask for: speech-to-text is billed per audio
minute, synthesis per character, and the language model per token pair, where a
turn carries the system prompt, a short retrieval context, and the last few
messages of history.

Retrieval is the cheapest term by a wide margin because it is computed locally:
no network hop, no per-request fee, and a fixed cost per turn that does not
grow with traffic the way a hosted embedding API would. What retrieval does
affect is the prompt bill, since every character of cited evidence is repeated
input tokens on all later turns of the session.

That is why the context builder caps quotes, keeps one chunk per document, and
refuses to answer when the evidence is weak: a wrong answer costs a human
follow-up, and the handling cost of one misrouted call dwarfs the entire model
spend of the automated one.

The console reports cost per resolved call, cost per minute of audio, and the
retrieval share of prompt tokens, so a team can see whether quality or spend
should move next.
