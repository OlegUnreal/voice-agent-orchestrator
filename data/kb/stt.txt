Title: Streaming speech-to-text behaviour
Tags: speech to text, transcription, vad, partials, accuracy
Updated: 2026-05-25

Transcription is streaming. Audio chunks of 20 milliseconds are sent as they
are captured and partial hypotheses are emitted as the speaker talks, so the
agent can start planning before the utterance ends. A final transcript is
produced when the voice-activity detector closes the utterance.

Utterance closing is driven by a trailing-silence window that adapts to the
conversation: 400 milliseconds during a greeting, 700 milliseconds when the
caller is reading a long identifier such as an account number. Too short a
window cuts numbers in half; too long adds latency to every reply.

Accuracy is reported per tenant as word error rate on sampled human-reviewed
calls. The current production figure sits near 6 percent on clean audio and
rises on narrowband trunks. Domain biasing lifts it: registering a list of
product names, plan codes and account vocabulary reduces errors on those terms
by roughly a third without retraining anything.

Disfluencies are stripped before the text reaches the agent, timestamps are
attached to every word, and the raw audio is retained separately from the
transcript so a disputed utterance can be re-listened to inside the retention
window.
